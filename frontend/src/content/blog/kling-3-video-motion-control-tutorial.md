To animate an AI influencer image with Kling 3.0, upload your static image to an AI video generator equipped with Kling. Use the Motion Brush to highlight areas like hair or clothing, and draw directional vectors (arrows) indicating where they should move. Combine this with a prompt like "woman walking forward, cinematic slow motion" to produce a highly realistic, viral-ready video clip.

Static images don't go viral on TikTok or Instagram Reels. The algorithm demands motion. Until recently, turning an AI-generated image into a high-quality video resulted in morphed faces, weird background warping, and unusable glitches. Kling 3.0 Pro changes everything.

While models like Sora 2 Pro and Seedance 2.0 are incredibly powerful for text-to-video, Kling 3.0 Pro (and specifically its Motion Control feature) is currently the undisputed king of Image-to-Video (I2V).

When you are managing an AI influencer, you already have a perfect, face-locked static image. You don't want the video AI to "guess" what your character looks like—you just want it to make the image move. Kling excels at this.

Garbage in, garbage out. If your base image has weird anatomical flaws (six fingers, a third leg merging into a chair), Kling will animate those flaws beautifully.

This is where the magic happens. In your Kling Video interface, you'll see a tool called Motion Brush or Motion Control.

When using Image-to-Video, your prompt should only describe the action and camera movement. The AI already knows what the character looks like from the image. Do not waste prompt space describing their clothes.

❌ Bad Prompt:

"A blonde woman in a red jacket standing in a city."
            
            (The AI already sees she is blonde and in a city. This prompts no action).

✅ Good Prompt:

"Subject turns head slowly to smile at the camera. Gentle breeze blowing hair. Cinematic slow pan right."

                What is Kling Motion Control?
